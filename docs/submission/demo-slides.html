The problem

Five hazards. One closed cabin. A late reply.

Radiation

Beyond the magnetosphere, dose rate rises. The crew needs a shelter cue tied to a real solar notice.

Isolation

Sleep, mood, and routine slip. The crew needs a check that is not a ranking.

Distance

Mars delay is 4 to 24 minutes. The decision on board happens first.

Gravity and the closed cabin

Heart, eyes, bone, muscle, and air change together. One person cannot watch every stream.

PULSARNASA Human Research Program hazards2 / 7

The solution

Each role sees only what they must act on.

Astronaut

Dials, body map, kits, forecasts in plain words. Apply, mark done, or escalate.

Crew surgeon

Whole crew, files, live charts. Validates, replaces, or escalates. Ultrasound in six locked steps.

Houston

Compressed report, ultrasound stills, queued recommendation for the next uplink.

A red alarm is one beep plus a banner. It cannot be muted. Orange stays visual. If the ship server is silent, the tablet keeps a labeled simulator.

NASA data

DONKI on the ship. HRP on the map. Vitals are a demo.

DONKI notifications

The server calls api.nasa.gov for the last 7 days. The key never reaches the browser. The card says Connected, Cache, or Offline. It is a solar reference, not a crew dose.

Human Research Program

The five hazards structure the body map and the ultrasound zones: heart, eye (SANS), lungs, kidneys, muscle.

Real astronaut records are private. On-screen vitals are fictional. Earth thresholds (CDC, AHA) are cabin watch points, not flight limits.

PULSARAt least one NASA data product: DONKI4 / 7

What the judges can open

A working prototype, labeled where it is simulated.

  • Astronaut home. Five dials, 3D body, to-do list, live heart-rate and SpO2 chart.
  • Forecasts. Observed line, three dashed heart-rate models (XGBoost, LSTM, Prophet), time to threshold.
  • Kits. Glucose, blood pressure, temperature, mood. Lamp, action, and who is notified.
  • Crew surgeon. Priority case, files, six-step ultrasound, journal.
  • Houston. Report, crew table, forecast summary, uplink queue, contact windows.

Impact and the next step

The crew acts in the cabin. Houston reviews the short report.

Now

An astronaut sees a rising resting pulse and the next action, without waiting for a 20-minute reply.

On board

The crew surgeon confirms or replaces that action and attaches an ultrasound only when it is needed.

Next

Replace the demo series and the Earth thresholds with each astronaut’s pre-flight baseline.
